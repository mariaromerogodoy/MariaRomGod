NYC Sound

Making the noise of New York City easier to see, understand, and navigate

UI/UX DesignData VisualizationService Design
01 / The challenge

NYC is vibrant. NYC is loud.

New York City has a lot of noise, and information about complaints is publicly available. But searching through large datasets can make it hard for residents to understand where complaints happen, what kinds of noise are reported, and whether a case is open or closed.

For a Figma design challenge, I wanted to turn that information into a more approachable experience: a map that lets people explore patterns across the city and find complaints relevant to their own neighborhood.

02 / Research

Learning what happens after someone reports noise.

Instead of only looking at the dataset, I visited an NYPD precinct to better understand how noise complaints are received and handled. I focused my questions on the journey from filing a complaint to resolving it, the challenges involved, and how decisions about case status are made.

That research helped me prioritize information people may look for when exploring a case: complaint type, time of day, concentration of reports, and open or closed status.

03 / Visual language

Not just color. Shape, too.

Because color alone isn't accessible to everyone, I paired each complaint category with a unique shape. Inspired by music and sound, the symbols use a soft blur to suggest the way noise moves and spreads through space.

Six categories: residential, street/sidewalk, vehicle, construction, commercial, and other

04 / Making sense of the data

Filters that make the map feel manageable

I designed filtering around four questions: What type of noise? When was it reported? How many complaints were filed at the address? And is the case open or closed?

People can also search for a specific address or neighborhood, moving between a citywide view and a more personal one.

Filtering reduces the markers displayed so particular complaint types and case statuses are easier to compare

05 / Case details

From a dot on the map to a case you can understand.

Clicking a marker brings the user closer to a specific complaint. The details view shows an address, status, case number, time of day, complaint type, and the count of complaints at that location.

The goal is to connect the broader data visualization to information that actually matters to someone in the neighborhood.

06 / Reporting flow

Making the next step clear.

I also designed a reporting entry point that gathers a noise category, location, time, and short description. The interface makes it clear that a report is not submitted on this website: people must continue to the official NYC 311 portal to review and submit.

This separates exploring public information from filing an official complaint, while making the transition between the two understandable.

07 / Final thoughts

Design can make complex information feel more human.

NYC Sound brought together my interests in data visualization, accessibility, and product design. I chose a website to make the information easier to reach, and a shape-based system to help more people recognize complaint categories.

The result is a concept for exploring NYC noise complaints that balances the energy of the city with a clear, functional interface. It was created for a design challenge; I haven't presented it as a live government reporting service or claimed user-testing outcomes.
